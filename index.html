03 · MATCHING METHOD

Compare observations without hiding their origin.

Sensors have different spatial footprints, sensitivities and overpass times. The application preserves these metadata with each point and separates observation counts from an interpretation as nearby events.

1Normalize

WGS84 coordinates, UTC date and time, sensor, confidence, brightness temperature and FRP.

2Preserve provenance

Each observation retains its satellite, instrument, product and download date.

3Compare series

Counts remain separated by month, year, area and sensor to avoid a false equivalence between resolutions.

4Document limitations

An absence of signal may reflect clouds, overpass time or missing coverage.

ⓘThe prototype's spatial and temporal matching is an exploratory aid. For published scientific trends, document resolution, overpasses, false-positive filtering and product changes.

CONFIGURATION

Connecting to NASA data

The FIRMS key is free and available by email from NASA. Keep it private and out of source code and Git.

  1. Get your key from the official NASA FIRMS website.
  2. In the project directory, copy .env.example to .env.
  3. Set FIRMS_MAP_KEY=your_key, then restart the server.

Open-Meteo weather and geocoding do not require a key for non-commercial use. Places are searched via Open-Meteo; map framing is indicative. Draw an area to select the analysis extent precisely.

ABOUT

See the signals, understand the risk.

Red/orange points are satellite thermal detections, not exact fire boundaries. Satellites can miss fires or detect them after their pass.

The blue corridor is an experimental visualization of wind and weather conditions (temperature, humidity, rain). It is not a physical spread simulation: terrain, vegetation, fuel and interventions are not yet considered.

Use official alerts and emergency services for operational decisions. Weather conditions come from Open-Meteo; detections come from NASA FIRMS.
